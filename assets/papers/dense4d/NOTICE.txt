Method facts: Riza Alp Guler, Dense4D: Pixel-Level 4D Human Correspondence, arXiv:2610.22325v1, Sections 3.1-3.4; https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22325v1#S3 .
Monocular RGB observations of one person, person masks and focal lengths define crop-aware inputs. Joint feed-forward heads predict Human Point Maps (HPMs) in the first observation's reference-time camera coordinates and per-frame depth, both with normalized scale. HPM nearest-neighbour matches establish target-frame corresponding pixels; each target's own depth lifts its match to a 3D camera-space position.
The reference surface is that person's first-input pose, not a shared canonical body template. Point samples are an explanatory surface depiction, not a watertight mesh, Gaussian avatar, measured scan, numeric HPM or exact prediction. Coordinate colors indicate shared surface position; discrete markers identify matching wrist, shoulder and knee points. The lower temporal poses are deliberately laid out side by side for explanation; their spacing and connecting curves are not estimated world-space displacement or measured trajectories. Grayscale maps schematically illustrate normalized optical-axis depth, not appearance/normal maps or metric distances.

Absolute body size and camera distance are not recovered; world placement requires separate scene depth and camera poses.
